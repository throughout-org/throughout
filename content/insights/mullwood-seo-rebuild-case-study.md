Müllwood is a Sri Lankan curly-hair brand with an ecommerce store and a specialist audience. The initial problem was not a lack of products or brand knowledge. Search systems could not reliably interpret the store.

The project ran from July to September 2026 and addressed technical cleanup, page structure, metadata, images, content and measurement. This case study reports implementation coverage documented by the project; it does not invent traffic or revenue outcomes that were not recorded.

## The starting point

The audit found missing structured data, no purposeful search titles or keyword targeting across the priority pages, 671 images without useful alternative text, one existing blog article, an indexed demonstration page and a malicious script in the blog area.

These were different classes of problem. Treating them as one generic “SEO score” would have hidden the dependencies:

- Security and unwanted content needed immediate attention.
- Indexing and template problems affected how the store was interpreted.
- Product and category pages needed distinct search intent.
- Images required accessible descriptions in the right context.
- Measurement needed to exist before longer-term outcomes could be evaluated.

## We fixed the foundation before scaling content

Publishing more articles onto an unclear or compromised foundation would have increased the amount of content to maintain without resolving the underlying problem.

The implementation established structured-data coverage across the intended templates, created SEO titles and keyword direction for 20 priority pages and products, corrected the documented missing image descriptions, and removed technical obstacles identified during the audit.

The team also added Google Analytics and Search Console, Bing and IndexNow support, an `llms.txt` file and a Google Business Profile. Each has a different purpose; none guarantees rankings or AI citations by itself.

## Content was organized around customer questions

The store grew from one blog article to 13, with three additional category pages. The important change was not simply publishing twelve more articles. The new structure created places to answer distinct questions about curly-hair needs, product categories and buying decisions.

Content planning should prevent category pages and articles from competing for the same intent. A category page helps someone evaluate products; an educational article explains a problem or method. Internal links then help readers move between learning and shopping without forcing every page to do both jobs.

## Implementation coverage is not the same as business impact

The project can accurately report changes such as structured-data coverage moving from 0% to the intended templates, 671 missing image descriptions being addressed, and the planned page and article work being published.

Those figures describe delivery. Search impressions, qualified visits and sales require observation over an appropriate period and should be compared against a baseline. Algorithm changes, seasonality and other marketing activity also affect the outcome.

## What this case demonstrates

The useful pattern is sequencing:

1. Remove security and indexing risks.
2. Clarify templates, entities and page intent.
3. establish metadata and structured data.
4. Fix accessible image context.
5. Build content around evidenced customer questions.
6. Connect measurement before judging the work.

An ecommerce SEO rebuild is not a collection of isolated tags. It is a system that helps customers and machines understand the catalogue, the expertise behind it and the route to a useful next step.
