Dedicated developer pricing is usually a monthly rate per person, set by role, seniority and the studio's location and overheads, plus any management or tooling fees. The headline rate matters less than what it includes, how the team is sized and what is excluded, so compare written quotes on a like-for-like basis.

We do not publish a rate card here, because honest pricing depends on the roles you need. This guide shows how pricing works so you can judge any quote, ours included.

## How is dedicated team pricing structured?

Most studios charge a fixed monthly fee per team member with a notice period. Some charge hourly with a monthly cap or bundle a delivery lead and QA. The fee covers salary, equipment, office, management and margin, while you direct the work and the studio handles hiring, payroll and continuity.

See [dedicated team vs project team](/insights/dedicated-team-vs-project-team-offshore) for when this model fits.

## What moves the price?

Five factors move a dedicated team quote more than any others: the seniority of the people, the mix of roles, how scarce the skills are, the size and length of the commitment, and the overlap hours you require with your time zone. Understanding these helps you see why two quotes differ. They are:

Five factors move a quote most:

1. **Seniority.** Senior engineers and architects cost more than mid-level developers.
2. **Role mix.** Developers, designers, QA, DevOps and project managers are priced differently.
3. **Skills.** Scarce skills such as machine learning or niche platforms carry a premium.
4. **Team size and term.** Larger teams and longer commitments can reduce the per-person rate.
5. **Overlap and coverage.** Fixed overlap hours with your time zone, or extra coverage, can change the price.

## What should a quote include?

A good quote lists the named roles and seniority, the monthly fee per role, billing terms, notice period, who owns the work, what tools and licences are included and how replacements and leave are handled. If the quote is one line, ask for the breakdown. Clarity on these items matters more than a lower number.

## What costs do founders forget?

Beyond the monthly fee, plan for your own management time, onboarding, tools and licences, cloud hosting, third-party services and a ramp-up period before the team reaches full speed. Rework from unclear requirements is the largest hidden cost, so invest in specs and a product owner.

For product-level budgeting, see [what a SaaS MVP costs offshore](/insights/saas-mvp-cost-offshore-2026).

## How do you compare two quotes fairly?

Compare cost per outcome, not rate per hour. Put both quotes into the same template: roles, seniority, monthly cost, overlap hours, notice period, ownership terms and exclusions. Add your estimated management time. Then ask what each studio would do if a key engineer left. The cheapest rate with weak continuity often costs more over a year.

## How do you control cost once the team is running?

Keep the team sized to the backlog, review capacity monthly, hold weekly demos so waste is visible and agree the notice period for scaling up or down. Start small, often two to four people, and grow when the backlog consistently exceeds capacity.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a dedicated developer cost per month?

It depends on seniority, skills and the studio. Ask for a written quote that names the role and level, and compare breakdowns rather than headline rates.

### Is a dedicated team cheaper than hiring locally?

Often yes on salary and overhead, but include management time, onboarding and the cost of rework. Compare total cost per outcome over a year.

### What is included in a dedicated team rate?

Usually the engineer's time, equipment, office, management overhead and the studio's margin. Confirm what is excluded, such as tools, licences and hosting.

### Can I scale a dedicated team up or down?

Usually yes, with a notice period. Agree the minimum term and notice before you sign.
