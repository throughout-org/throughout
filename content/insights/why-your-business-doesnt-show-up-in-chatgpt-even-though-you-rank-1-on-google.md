A business can perform well for an important Google query and still be absent from an answer generated by ChatGPT, Perplexity or another AI-assisted search product. That does not prove the business has been penalized, and it does not mean conventional SEO has stopped working.

The systems, indexes, retrieval methods and timing differ. Generated answers also vary between prompts and runs. The useful response is a diagnostic process, not a promise that one markup change will force a citation.

## First, confirm that you are testing the right thing

“Does ChatGPT know my brand?” and “Does ChatGPT search cite my current website?” are different questions.

A model may recall older information from training, retrieve current web results, or answer without searching. When testing visibility:

1. Use a search-enabled experience.
2. Ask a realistic buyer question, not only your brand name.
3. Check whether web search was used.
4. Open the listed sources.
5. Repeat the test with a small, consistent prompt set.
6. Record the date and engine.

OpenAI cautions that search results and citations can be incomplete or wrong. One response is an observation, not a market-share measurement.

## Check whether the crawler can reach the site

For ChatGPT search eligibility, OpenAI says sites should allow `OAI-SearchBot` and permit traffic from its published IP ranges. Access can be blocked by more than `robots.txt`: a CDN, firewall, bot-management service or rate limit may reject the crawler.

Verify:

- The important URL returns HTTP 200 without requiring login.
- `robots.txt` does not block the relevant crawler.
- The page does not contain an unintended `noindex`.
- The canonical points to the correct URL.
- Important content appears in the rendered HTML.
- Internal links lead to the page.
- CDN and security logs do not show blocked requests.

Eligibility does not guarantee inclusion, but a blocked page cannot be retrieved from the live web.

## Check whether the page answers the actual prompt

A homepage may rank for a short brand or service keyword while offering little support for a detailed recommendation question.

Compare the prompt with the page:

- Does the page clearly state who the service is for?
- Does it explain availability, limitations, location and pricing where appropriate?
- Does it answer the comparison criteria a buyer would use?
- Are important facts written as text rather than embedded in an image?
- Is the information current?

Do not add dozens of near-duplicate FAQ paragraphs just to capture prompt variations. Add information because it helps a reader make the decision represented by the prompt.

## Check entity clarity and consistency

A retrieval system encounters information about a business across its website and other public sources. Conflicting names, URLs, locations or product descriptions make that information harder to reconcile.

Audit maintained sources such as:

- The website and contact page
- Organization structured data
- Google Business Profile, where applicable
- Product documentation
- Relevant professional or industry profiles
- Reputable directory entries the business actually controls
- Press or partner pages

Correct genuine inconsistencies. Do not create a large volume of artificial profiles or mentions. Google explicitly warns that inauthentic mentions are not a useful AI-search strategy.

## Check whether independent sources exist

A company’s website is naturally self-authored. For recommendation questions, systems may retrieve reviews, reporting, directories, community discussions or other independent sources.

That does not justify manufacturing reviews or paying for low-quality placements. It means the business should earn and maintain verifiable public evidence through good work: accurate customer reviews, partner documentation, useful open-source projects, original research, expert contributions and legitimate coverage.

If no independent source discusses the business, the right fix is not a technical trick. It is building a reputation that produces credible references.

## Check content quality and freshness

Generated answers often address specific, current questions. A site made entirely of broad marketing pages may not contain the facts needed to support them.

Prioritize content that provides first-hand value:

- A tested implementation guide
- Current product documentation
- A transparent buyer guide
- A method and dataset behind original research
- A case study with approved, verifiable evidence
- A clear explanation of limitations as well as capabilities

Show a meaningful updated date when the content has actually been reviewed. Do not change a date automatically to make old information look fresh.

## Check structured data, but do not overstate it

Structured data can clarify visible facts and make a page eligible for supported search features. It is not a special switch for AI citations.

Use appropriate Organization, Product, Article or LocalBusiness markup only where it matches the page. Validate it and keep it consistent with the visible content. Google states that no special schema is required for its AI features.

## Measure visibility as a pattern

Create prompt groups around actual audience needs:

- Category discovery
- Problem and solution questions
- Comparisons
- Local recommendations
- Product capability questions
- Implementation and pricing questions

For each run, record the mention, wording, cited URL and referral traffic where available. Also monitor branded search, organic landing pages and conversions. AI visibility without relevance or useful action is not a business outcome.

## A sensible order of work

1. Fix crawler and indexing blockers.
2. Correct contradictory business information.
3. Improve pages that already address valuable buyer questions.
4. Add missing first-hand content.
5. Earn legitimate independent validation.
6. Measure a stable prompt set over time.

This order protects the work you already do for conventional search while expanding how it can be discovered. It also avoids spending months on speculative “GEO tactics” while a firewall blocks the crawler or a key product page lacks basic facts.
