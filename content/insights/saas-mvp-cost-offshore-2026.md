The cost of a SaaS MVP built offshore is mainly team size multiplied by time multiplied by blended rate. A narrow MVP with one core workflow costs far less than a multi-role platform with payments, integrations and reporting. Any single price quoted without a scope is a guess.

Rather than quote a number that would be wrong for most readers, this guide shows the formula, the drivers that move it, and a worked example with assumptions you can replace with your own.

## What is the formula?

The cost of a SaaS MVP is team size multiplied by time multiplied by a blended monthly rate, plus third-party costs and a contingency. Offshore rates vary by country, studio and seniority, so use written quotes. The structure holds whatever the rates are:

**Cost ≈ team × months × blended monthly rate, plus third-party costs and a contingency.**

## What drives the cost most?

Scope drives cost far more than the hourly rate. The biggest levers are how many user roles and workflows you build, how many integrations you need, how deep the design goes and how high the quality bar is. Eight drivers move the estimate most:

1. **Number of user roles.** Each role (admin, customer, staff) needs its own screens, permissions and tests.
2. **Core workflows.** One workflow done well is an MVP. Five is a product.
3. **Integrations.** Payments, email, accounting, calendars and CRMs each add build and test time.
4. **Design depth.** A clean, standard component set is cheaper than bespoke interaction design.
5. **Platforms.** Web only, or web plus mobile, changes the team and the timeline.
6. **Data and compliance.** Handling payments, health or children's data adds requirements.
7. **AI features.** Model calls, evaluation and guardrails add engineering and running costs.
8. **Quality bar.** Automated tests and monitoring cost time now and save more later.

## What does a worked example look like?

As an illustration, take a B2B SaaS with sign-up, one core workflow, a dashboard, Stripe subscriptions and email notifications. A small team over roughly 12 weeks is a reasonable planning assumption. The table lists the assumptions, which you should replace with figures from your own proposals.

| Item | Assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Team | 1 product designer, 2 developers, QA part-time, delivery lead part-time |
| Duration | Around 12 weeks from kickoff to launch |
| Effort | Roughly 7 to 8 person-months of delivery effort |
| Cost | Effort × your agreed blended monthly rate |
| Add on | Hosting, email, monitoring, domain, app-store fees |
| Contingency | 15 to 20 percent for discovered work |

These are planning assumptions, not a quote. Replace each with figures from your proposals, and expect your own scope to move them up or down.

## What do founders forget to budget for?

Founders most often forget discovery and design, third-party services, post-launch support, their own time and basic legal documents. Each can add meaningful cost or delay if it is not planned for early. Budget for these items alongside the build itself:

- **Discovery and design before build.** Skipping it moves cost into rework.
- **Third-party services** such as email, SMS, maps, AI model usage and monitoring.
- **Post-launch support** for bugs, hosting and small improvements.
- **Your own time.** Fast answers keep the schedule and the cost down.
- **Legal basics** such as terms, privacy policy and contracts.

## How do you keep MVP cost down without cutting quality?

You keep MVP cost down by cutting scope, not quality. Choose one role and one workflow, reuse proven components for sign-in and billing, defer internal tooling and build only what produces the evidence you need. Five habits do most of the work:

1. Pick **one user role and one workflow** for release one.
2. Use proven components for authentication, billing and email.
3. Defer admin tooling you can handle with a database console at first.
4. Decide what you will measure, then build only what produces that evidence.
5. Review working software weekly so mistakes are cheap.

## Should you choose a project price or a monthly team?

A fixed-scope project suits a well-defined first release, because price and scope are agreed up front. A monthly dedicated team suits a roadmap that will keep changing, because you pay for capacity rather than a fixed outcome. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to build a SaaS MVP?

It depends on scope, team and rates. Estimate with team × months × blended rate, then add third-party costs and contingency. Narrow, single-workflow MVPs cost a fraction of multi-role platforms.

### Is building an MVP offshore cheaper?

Often, because offshore rates are lower than local agencies in Australia, Europe and the Gulf. The saving only holds if scope is controlled and feedback is fast, since rework erases rate differences.

### How long does an MVP take to build?

Commonly two to four months for a focused scope, depending on complexity and how fast decisions are made. Be wary of any fixed promise made before scoping.

### What is the cheapest way to validate an idea?

A prototype or landing page test can be cheaper than code. Build the MVP when you need real usage data.
