Retention marketing starts with a simple advantage: the customer already knows the business. The challenge is reaching that customer with a relevant reason to return without turning the relationship into indiscriminate messaging.

This case study documents a small Comflo campaign shown on the Throughout website. It reached 200 existing customers through WhatsApp, used LKR 1,400 in campaign spend and recorded more than LKR 40,000 in revenue within 24 hours. That is a documented 28.6× return on the stated messaging spend for this campaign—not a promise that another campaign will produce the same result.

## The opportunity was in the existing customer list

The campaign did not begin by buying a new audience. It used people who had already interacted with the business. That changed the job from introducing an unknown brand to giving previous customers a timely reason to return.

Before contacting an existing audience, a team should still confirm:

- How consent and messaging preferences were collected
- Whether the audience is relevant to the offer
- Which customers should be excluded
- Who will answer replies and order questions
- How orders and revenue will be attributed

The customer list is not automatically a campaign strategy. Segmentation, timing and operational readiness still matter.

## The campaign kept the test deliberately small

Reaching 200 customers created a bounded test. The team could observe response volume, fulfilment pressure and order value without immediately scaling to the entire customer base.

A useful pilot should define its audience, offer, send time, measurement window and stopping conditions before launch. Otherwise, a positive result can be expanded faster than the business can fulfil it.

## Revenue alone does not describe the whole result

The documented comparison is messaging spend against revenue recorded during the following 24 hours. It does not claim that messaging spend was the only campaign cost or that every purchase was caused solely by the message.

A fuller evaluation should also consider:

- Gross margin on the resulting orders
- Discounts and delivery subsidies
- Staff time spent answering and fulfilling orders
- Cancellations, refunds and returns
- Customers who opted out or complained
- Whether purchases would have happened without the campaign

These limitations do not erase the result. They make the result more useful by showing what it does and does not prove.

## The operational workflow mattered

A campaign can succeed at generating replies and still fail operationally. Incoming conversations need ownership, customer context and a clear path from enquiry to confirmed order.

That is where a connected commerce workflow becomes valuable. The team should be able to see who is replying, whether an order exists, its current status and what the customer previously purchased. If those details live in separate chats and spreadsheets, campaign volume increases the risk of duplicated replies and missed changes.

## What we would repeat

For a similar retention test, we would retain four principles:

1. Start with a small, relevant segment.
2. Use one clear offer and measurement window.
3. Prepare the response and fulfilment workflow before sending.
4. Report revenue alongside costs, margin, opt-outs and operational exceptions.

The lesson is not that every WhatsApp campaign produces exceptional returns. It is that existing customer relationships can be tested responsibly when the audience, message, order workflow and measurement are connected.
