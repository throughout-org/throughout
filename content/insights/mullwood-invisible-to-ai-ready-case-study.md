In 90 days, from July to September 2026, we rebuilt Müllwood's ecommerce store so that Google and AI assistants could read it: structured data went from 0% to 100%, 20 pages and products gained SEO titles and keywords, 671 images gained alt text, and the blog grew from 1 to 13 articles.

This is the narrative version of the work. The full numbers are on the [Müllwood case study page](/case-studies/mullwood). We report delivery, not traffic or revenue, because those were not part of the recorded results.

## What was wrong at the start?

Müllwood is Sri Lanka's first curly-hair brand, with a specialist audience and real products, but the store gave search systems almost nothing to work with. There was no structured data, no purposeful titles or keywords on priority pages, 671 images without useful alternative text, one blog article, an indexed demonstration page and a malicious script in the blog area.

## Why did we fix the foundation before publishing?

Publishing more articles onto an unclear or compromised site would have added content to maintain without fixing the cause. So month one removed the security and indexing risks, then established titles, metadata and structured data. Content followed once the templates could carry it. This is the same sequencing we describe in [how to prioritise SEO issues after an audit](/insights/how-to-prioritize-seo-issues-after-an-audit).

## What did the structured data change?

Structured data states, in a machine-readable form, what a page is: a product, a category, an article or a business. We added schema.org markup across the intended templates, matched to what is visible on each page. It does not guarantee a ranking or an AI citation, but it removes guesswork about what the store sells and who is behind it.

## How did we choose the content?

We built content around the questions curly-haired customers actually ask, and gave each product category its own page so articles and category pages did not compete for the same intent. The blog grew from 1 to 13 articles and three category pages were added.

Each article opens with a direct answer, which suits both featured snippets and AI assistants. Our [content brief method](/insights/how-to-write-a-content-brief-for-search-intent) describes the process.

## What did we add for AI assistants?

We added an llms.txt file, confirmed that AI crawlers could access the site, set up Bing and IndexNow alongside Google Search Console, and created a Google Business Profile. Each has a different job, and none guarantees a citation. They make the site eligible and easy to understand. For the full reasoning, read [GEO explained](/insights/geo-explained-get-recommended-by-chatgpt-gemini).

## What did we measure, and what did we not claim?

We measured delivery: structured-data coverage, pages with titles, images fixed and articles published. Rankings, traffic and AI citations need a baseline and time, so we did not claim them. If you are planning your own measurement, see [how to measure SEO changes after implementation](/insights/how-to-measure-seo-changes-after-implementation).

## What would we repeat?

Five practices from this project are worth repeating on any ecommerce store: secure the site and clean the index first, fix templates before scaling content, give each page one clear intent, lead with the answer and agree what you will measure up front. They apply regardless of platform.

- Clean up security and indexing first.
- Fix templates before scaling content.
- Give every page one clear search intent.
- Write the answer first, then the explanation.
- Agree what you will measure before the work starts.
