Before you sign with an offshore studio, ask twelve questions covering who does the work, who owns it, how you will communicate, how quality and security are handled, and how you can leave. Good answers are specific and written down. Vague answers are the most reliable warning sign.

Use the list below as a script on discovery calls. It applies to any country; see our [guide to offshore development in Sri Lanka](/insights/offshore-software-development-sri-lanka-guide) for market specifics.

## Who will actually do the work?

The people doing the work matter more than the company name. Ask who is assigned, how long they have worked together and what happens if someone leaves, and insist on meeting the lead engineer before you sign. Three questions cover this ground.

**1. Who is on the team, and can I meet them?** Ask for names, roles and experience of the people assigned, not the sales lead. A good answer introduces the lead engineer before you sign.

**2. Is the team dedicated to my project, or shared?** Shared capacity is fine if agreed, but you should know it. Ask what happens when someone leaves.

**3. Do you use subcontractors?** If yes, ask which parts, and whether they are under the same contract and security rules.

## Who owns the work, and how is it protected?

Ownership and security should be settled in writing before any work starts. You should own the code, designs and content, confidentiality should be covered by an NDA, and access should follow least-privilege rules. Two questions test whether a studio takes this seriously.

**4. Who owns the code, designs and content?** The answer should be you, written into the contract before work starts. Ask about third-party licences and open-source obligations.

**5. How do you handle NDAs, access and security?** Expect an NDA before you share details, least-privilege access, separate environments and a plan for secrets and backups.

## How will you work together?

Communication is where most offshore projects succeed or fail. Agree fixed overlap hours, a visible way to track progress and a clear process for changing scope, and expect written updates as well as calls. Daily habits matter more than tools. Three questions test this.

**6. What time zones and overlap hours do you offer?** Ask for fixed overlap hours and response times. For Australia, Europe and the Gulf from Sri Lanka, see our [overlap table](/insights/offshore-software-development-sri-lanka-guide).

**7. How will I see progress?** Good answers include weekly working builds, a shared board, written updates and direct access to the engineers.

**8. How do you handle changes to scope?** Look for a clear change process, not a promise that nothing will change.

## How is quality managed?

Quality comes from process, not promises. Ask how code is reviewed and tested, what happens when defects appear after launch, and whether you can inspect comparable live work and speak to a past client. Two questions help you judge this properly.

**9. What is your testing and review process?** Ask about code review, automated tests, QA, staging and how defects are handled after launch.

**10. Can I see comparable work and talk to a client?** Ask for live products and a reference. Studios that run their own products, as we do with Rankout and Checkout, can show how they maintain software over time.

## What are the commercial terms?

Commercial clarity protects both sides. Ask what the price includes and excludes, how estimates are built, and how you can scale down, switch or exit, including handover of code and documentation. Two questions cover the terms that cause the most disputes later.

**11. How is pricing structured, and what is not included?** Ask about hosting, third-party services, post-launch support and estimates. Compare proposals line by line. See [what an MVP costs offshore](/insights/saas-mvp-cost-offshore-2026).

**12. How can I scale down, switch or leave?** Ask about notice periods, handover, documentation and access to repositories if you end the contract. A confident studio makes leaving easy.

## How do you score the answers?

Rate each answer from 0 to 2: 0 for vague, 1 for acceptable, 2 for specific and documented. Anything below 1 on ownership, security or exit terms is a reason to pause. Then decide on the model with [dedicated team vs project team](/insights/dedicated-team-vs-project-team-offshore).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I choose an offshore development company?

Compare written proposals, meet the actual team, review live work, confirm ownership and security terms and run a short paid pilot before a long commitment.

### What should an offshore development contract include?

Scope, price and payment terms, ownership of code and designs, confidentiality, security obligations, change handling, notice periods and handover on exit.

### Is a fixed-price or monthly team model better?

Fixed price suits a defined project. A monthly dedicated team suits continuing work. Pick based on how well you can define scope.

### How can I reduce risk with a new offshore partner?

Start with a small paid pilot of two to four weeks, review working software weekly and keep repository access in your own account.
