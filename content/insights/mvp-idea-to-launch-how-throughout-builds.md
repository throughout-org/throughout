A focused SaaS MVP typically moves through five phases over roughly 12 weeks: discovery, design, build, test and launch. Ninety days is a planning frame, not a promise. The real timeline depends on scope, integrations and how quickly decisions are made, and we set it in the proposal after discovery.

This is the sequence we follow, based on building our own products, including [Rankout](https://rankout.app/), Checkout and Comflo, and client work.

## Phase 1: Discovery (weeks 1 to 2)

**Goal:** agree what the MVP must prove.

- Define the user, the problem and the single decision the MVP should inform.
- List the workflows and cut everything that is not needed to test that decision.
- Surface risks early: integrations, data, compliance, cost of third-party services.
- Agree success measures and what "launched" means.

**Output:** a written scope, a prioritised backlog and an estimate with its assumptions.

## Phase 2: Design (weeks 2 to 4)

**Goal:** make the product understandable before it is built.

- Map the key flows and sketch low-fidelity layouts.
- Design the core screens and states: empty, loading, error and success.
- Build a clickable prototype and test it with two or three target users.
- Prepare handoff using our [design handoff checklist](/insights/design-handoff-checklist-for-development).

**Output:** approved designs and a prototype.

## Phase 3: Build (weeks 4 to 10)

**Goal:** ship working software every week.

- Set up the repository, environments and deployment pipeline first.
- Build the thinnest end-to-end slice, then widen it.
- Review a working build with you every week.
- Keep a short backlog and agree changes explicitly.

**Output:** a staging environment you can use.

## Phase 4: Test and harden (weeks 10 to 11)

**Goal:** make it safe to put in front of real users.

- Fix defects found in QA and in your own testing.
- Check performance, security basics, analytics and backups.
- Prepare support routes, terms and a privacy policy.

**Output:** a release candidate.

## Phase 5: Launch and learn (weeks 11 to 12 and after)

**Goal:** get real usage and start learning.

- Deploy to production with monitoring in place.
- Onboard the first users and watch how they actually behave.
- Review the evidence against the success measures from phase 1.
- Decide what to build next, and whether to continue as a [dedicated team](/insights/dedicated-team-vs-project-team-offshore).

## What can make it longer?

Timelines slip for ordinary reasons rather than technical ones. The usual causes are scope that grows after design approval, slow feedback, unclear ownership of decisions, poorly documented integrations, compliance work discovered late and content or data that arrive after the build needs them.

- Scope that grows after design is approved.
- Slow feedback or no single decision-maker.
- Integrations with systems that have poor documentation.
- Compliance work discovered late.
- Content, legal copy or data that arrive late.

## What do you need to provide?

You need to provide a product owner with time each week, access to a few sample users, brand assets and any accounts or data the product depends on. Fast, specific answers are the cheapest way to protect a schedule, because every day a decision waits is a day the build waits too.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to build an MVP?

A focused SaaS MVP commonly takes two to four months. We agree a timeline in the proposal after discovery rather than promise one in advance.

### Can an MVP be built in 90 days?

Often, when scope is narrow and decisions are quick. Treat 90 days as a plan to manage against, not a guarantee.
