A content brief should help a writer produce the most useful page for a specific audience decision. It should not be a rigid outline assembled from competitor headings or a target word count.

## State the audience and decision

Describe who is searching, what situation they are in and what they should understand or decide after reading. “People interested in SEO” is too broad. “An agency lead deciding which audit issues enter the next development sprint” gives the writer a real problem.

## Define the primary intent

Clarify whether the page should explain, compare, help complete a task or support a purchase investigation. Mixed intent is sometimes valid, but one page should not attempt to be a beginner definition, technical manual, pricing page and sales pitch simultaneously.

Record adjacent intents that deserve separate pages so the new article does not overlap existing content.

## Document current search results

Review the result types, page formats, recurring subquestions and sources that appear for representative queries. Note what ranking pages answer well and what remains unclear, outdated or unsupported.

The objective is not to copy the current results. It is to understand the minimum expectations and identify where firsthand experience or clearer evidence can improve the answer.

## Specify evidence requirements

List the primary documentation, research, internal expertise or approved case evidence the writer should use. Mark claims that require dates, geography or qualification.

If reliable evidence is unavailable, change or remove the claim. Do not add a statistic because the introduction feels empty.

## Build the answer structure

Start with a direct response to the main question, then order sections around the reader's next questions. Include constraints, exceptions and practical steps where they matter.

The brief can suggest headings, but the writer should be free to improve the structure when research reveals a better sequence.

## Plan internal links and the next step

Identify earlier articles that supply prerequisite knowledge and product or service pages that genuinely help the reader continue. Internal links should explain the relationship rather than use repetitive exact-match anchors.

Define a CTA appropriate to the intent. An informational article may invite the reader to use a checklist or inspect a related workflow before asking for a demo.

## Define success before publishing

Record the target query group, current performance, intended conversion and review period. Also check indexability, metadata, canonical URL, author, updated date and structured data.

A strong brief protects usefulness. It gives the writer evidence and direction while leaving enough room to produce an article that sounds informed rather than assembled.
