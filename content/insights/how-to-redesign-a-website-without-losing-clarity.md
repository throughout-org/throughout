A redesign can make a website look more current while making it harder to understand. Navigation labels change, useful pages disappear, headings become slogans and conversion paths are rebuilt without a baseline.

The safest redesign process begins with what people need to find and do—not with a new visual direction.

## Inventory the current website

List indexable URLs, navigation paths, page purpose, owners, traffic and meaningful conversions. Mark content that is accurate, outdated, duplicated or legally required.

Do not delete a page merely because it looks old. Determine whether people or search engines still rely on it, and plan an appropriate replacement or redirect when its URL must change.

## Identify the priority tasks

Interview stakeholders, review search queries and analytics, and observe representative users completing important tasks. A service website may need to help visitors understand fit, inspect evidence and contact the team. An ecommerce site has different priorities.

Turn those tasks into an information hierarchy before drawing page layouts.

## Write the page argument first

For each important page, define:

- Who it is for
- The question it answers
- The evidence it provides
- The action it supports
- The objections it must address

This prevents the visual design from becoming a sequence of attractive but interchangeable sections.

## Preserve useful search signals

Keep valuable URLs by default. Map unavoidable changes one-to-one, retain meaningful headings and metadata, and ensure important content remains available in rendered HTML.

Capture a crawl, analytics baseline and Search Console data before launch. After release, verify redirects, canonical tags, sitemap entries, structured data and indexability.

## Design with real content and constraints

Use actual service names, case-study evidence, form fields and realistic text lengths. Test mobile navigation, keyboard focus, zoom, contrast and error states while components are still easy to change.

## Measure clarity after launch

Check whether people reach priority pages, understand the offer and complete the intended next step. Combine analytics with session observation, support questions and short usability tests.

A successful redesign does not merely replace the previous appearance. It makes the business easier to understand while preserving the accumulated value of the existing website.
