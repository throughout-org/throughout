Customers do not need every small business to have an elaborate website. They do need enough reliable information to decide whether the business is real, relevant and safe to contact.

The previous version of this article used unsupported percentages about Sri Lankan consumer behaviour and SME website adoption. Those figures have been removed. The useful question is not whether an unverified share of businesses is “invisible.” It is whether your own customers can find and verify the information they need.

Use this checklist to review that experience.

## Can customers identify the business?

Your website and maintained public profiles should use a consistent:

- Business name
- Logo and visual identity
- Description of what you sell or provide
- Phone number and email address
- Physical address or service area, where relevant
- Opening or response hours
- Website domain

If the registered company name differs from the trading name, explain the relationship where customers would reasonably need it—for example in the footer, terms, invoices or payment instructions.

Inconsistent contact details create practical confusion. They can also make it harder for search systems to reconcile information about the same organization.

## Does the homepage answer basic questions quickly?

A first-time visitor should be able to understand:

1. What the business offers
2. Who it serves
3. Where it operates
4. How to enquire, visit or buy
5. What happens after taking that action

Avoid opening with abstract claims such as “transforming tomorrow through excellence.” Use the language customers use when explaining the need.

## Is there evidence behind the promise?

The right evidence depends on the business:

- Original product photographs
- Clear service examples
- Verifiable client or partner references used with permission
- A portfolio showing the actual work
- Named qualifications or licences where relevant
- Current policies
- Genuine reviews linked to their platform
- Accurate availability and pricing information

Do not invent testimonials, project outcomes or customer counts. A smaller amount of verifiable evidence is more valuable than a page of anonymous praise.

## Are contact and payment routes clear?

Display the channels the team actively monitors. A WhatsApp link is useful only if someone responds. A form should confirm that the message was received and set an honest expectation for a reply.

Before asking for payment, make clear:

- The legal or trading name receiving the payment
- What the customer is purchasing
- The total and currency
- Delivery or completion expectations
- Refund, return or cancellation terms
- How the customer can obtain support

For ecommerce, use a reputable payment flow and avoid requesting sensitive payment credentials through chat.

## Does the site work well on the customer’s phone?

Mobile quality is not simply fitting the page onto a smaller screen. Test whether a customer can:

- Read the primary text without zooming
- Use navigation and form controls
- Tap the correct phone or WhatsApp link
- Understand validation errors
- Load important pages on a slower connection
- Complete checkout without losing entered information
- Use the page with keyboard and assistive technology

Compress images, avoid unnecessary scripts and use real devices as well as browser simulations.

## Is the connection secure?

Serve the site over HTTPS and redirect HTTP consistently. Keep the application, plugins and dependencies updated. Limit administrative access and enable multi-factor authentication where available.

HTTPS protects the connection; it does not prove that every claim on a website is trustworthy. Present it as one necessary control rather than a trust badge that guarantees the business.

## Are privacy expectations explained?

Tell visitors what information is collected and why, particularly for forms, analytics, advertising pixels, accounts and payments. Collect only what is needed and restrict access internally.

If the business serves customers in multiple jurisdictions, obtain appropriate legal advice rather than copying another website’s privacy notice.

## Can search engines understand the business accurately?

Make important pages crawlable and internally linked. Use unique page titles and useful descriptions. Add Organization or LocalBusiness structured data only when it matches visible, current information.

For a location-based business, keep the website and maintained business profiles consistent. For a service-area company, describe the actual areas served instead of creating thin pages for every town.

## Is social media supporting or replacing the website?

Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp may be the main discovery or conversation channels for a business. They are valuable, but the business does not control their algorithms, account access or page format.

A website can act as the stable reference point for:

- Official contact details
- Services or catalogue information
- Policies
- Long-form explanations
- Search visibility
- Campaign landing pages
- Links to active social channels

This does not mean every business needs ecommerce or a large custom build. A focused, maintained site is better than a complex site that becomes outdated.

## Review the full customer journey

Ask someone unfamiliar with the business to complete a realistic task:

- Find whether the business offers a specific service.
- Check whether it serves their location.
- Verify how to contact it.
- Understand what information an enquiry requires.
- Find the return or cancellation policy.

Watch where they hesitate. Those moments are more useful than a generic “modern design” score.

## A minimum trustworthy website brief

For many small businesses, the first useful version includes:

- A clear homepage
- Focused service or product information
- About and ownership context
- Accurate contact details
- Relevant evidence
- Privacy and commercial policies
- Accessible mobile performance
- Search and analytics basics
- A maintenance owner

The objective is not to look bigger than the business is. It is to make the real business easier to understand and verify.
