A multi-brand SEO strategy fixes the foundations once, then gives each brand its own search intent and states in structured data how the brands relate. In month one for CoreConnect Distributions we did this across Aerix, Airmate and CoreConnect, and published eight answer-ready guides. This post explains the approach; the numbers are on the [case study page](/case-studies/coreconnect-group).

The results reported here describe delivery in the first month. Rankings and AI citations follow as search engines recrawl, so we have not claimed them.

## What was the starting point?

CoreConnect Distributions runs three sites: Aerix for business scenting, Airmate for home diffusers and the CoreConnect parent brand. Search engines and AI assistants had almost nothing to read. The sites had no SEO titles, no structured data, leftover template pages and duplicate URLs. Airmate had no AI Overview citations at the start.

## Why treat three sites as one project?

The three sites shared the same causes: missing titles, no schema and template leftovers. Fixing the pattern once and applying it three times was faster and more consistent than three separate projects. The brands still needed different content, because a business buyer of scenting for a hotel and a home shopper looking for a diffuser search for different things.

## How did we keep each brand's search intent separate?

Each brand keeps its own audience and keywords, so pages do not compete with each other. Aerix targets business scenting, Airmate targets home diffusers, and CoreConnect explains the group. We gave each page one clear purpose and avoided publishing near-duplicate content across the three domains, which would split relevance and confuse assistants.

## What did we fix first?

We started with the shared technical basics. Duplicate pages were redirected, leftover template pages removed and sitemaps submitted to Google. Then we wrote SEO titles and metas: Aerix URLs with titles and metas went from 0% to 90%, and Airmate pages and products with SEO titles went from 0 to 61.

## How did we state brand relationships in schema?

We added structured data stating that Aerix and Airmate belong to CoreConnect Distributions. Product and business schema covers all 59 Aerix products, FAQ schema runs across the group, and CoreConnect pages with structured data went from 0% to 100%. Explicit ownership markup removes guesswork for search engines and assistants.

## What content did we publish?

We published eight answer-ready guides across the three sites. Each opens with a direct answer under its heading, then adds detail, which suits snippets and AI assistants. We also described 246 images with alt text. For the method behind this, see [how to write a content brief around search intent](/insights/how-to-write-a-content-brief-for-search-intent).

## What did we not claim?

This was month one of a longer programme, so we report delivery, not traffic or citations. Search impressions, rankings and AI citations need a baseline and time, and other factors affect them. Months two and three build content and authority. Our guide on [measuring SEO changes](/insights/how-to-measure-seo-changes-after-implementation) explains how we separate delivery from outcomes.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you do SEO for multiple brands?

Fix the shared technical foundations once, give each brand its own audience and keywords, avoid duplicate content across sites and state brand relationships in structured data.

### Should related brands share one website or have separate sites?

It depends on the audiences and the brand strategy. Separate sites suit distinct audiences, as here. Whatever you choose, make the relationships clear in schema and internal links.

### Why add schema that links brands to a parent company?

It gives search engines and AI assistants an explicit statement of ownership, which reduces guesswork about which brand belongs to which company.

### How long until the results show?

Delivery metrics are immediate, but rankings and citations follow as search engines recrawl, usually over months. Track them against a baseline.
